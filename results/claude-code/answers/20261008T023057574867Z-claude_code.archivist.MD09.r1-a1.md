Here are the answers, all taken from Meta's filings.

**(a) Employees affected:** about **11,000**. The 8-K says the layoff is "anticipated to impact approximately 11,000 of our employees across our Family of Apps and Reality Labs segments." The attached message from Zuckerberg says "more than 11,000," about 13% of the team. ([8-K, Item 7.01](<url:mosaic-finance.com>); [Ex. 99.1](<url:mosaic-finance.com>))

**(b) Reaffirmed 2022 expense outlook:** **$85–87 billion**, unchanged. It included expected Q4 severance costs and about $900 million in charges for consolidating office facilities. ([8-K, Item 7.01](<url:mosaic-finance.com>))

**(c) Total costs and expenses for full year 2022:** **$87,665 million**. ([10-K, income statement](<url:mosaic-finance.com>))

**(d) Headcount at December 31, 2022:** **86,482**, up 20% from a year earlier. This number still includes most of the roughly 11,000 laid-off employees, who would be out of the headcount by the end of Q1 2023. ([10-K, MD&A](<url:mosaic-finance.com>))

**(e) Amount above the top of the range:** $87,665M − $87,000M = **$665 million**, which is my own calculation.

My reading, not something the filings say: part of the gap probably comes from a decision made after the outlook. In December 2022 Meta changed its data center plans, which led to **$1,341 million** in data center abandonment charges. These were part of $4,611 million in total 2022 restructuring charges. ([10-K, Note 3](<url:mosaic-finance.com>))
