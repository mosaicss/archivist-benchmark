In 2025 the F-35 brought in about **$20.3 billion** of Lockheed Martin's sales, which is 27% of total consolidated sales and 67% of Aeronautics segment sales.

**What the 10-K reports (filed 2026-01-29):**
- **F-35 share:** 27% of total consolidated sales and 67% of Aeronautics' sales in 2025 ([Item 1](<url:mosaic-finance.com>)). Note 3 calls the 27% "approximately" and gives 26% for both 2024 and 2023 ([Note 3](<url:mosaic-finance.com>)).
- **Aeronautics, 2025:** sales were **$30,257M** and operating profit was **$2,086M**, a 6.9% margin ([MD&A](<url:mosaic-finance.com>)).
- **Total consolidated sales, 2025:** **$75,048M** ([segment table](<url:mosaic-finance.com>)).

**My calculation (the filing doesn't state this figure):**
- 67% × $30,257M ≈ **$20,272M**
- 27% × $75,048M ≈ **$20,263M**

The two methods agree to within about $10M. The percentages are rounded, so treat the result as approximate.

The MD&A also says F-35 sales rose by about $1.9 billion in 2025 on higher production and sustainment volume. Aeronautics' operating profit still fell 17%, mainly because of a $950 million reach-forward loss on a classified program.
